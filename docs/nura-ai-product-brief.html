What Nura AI is

Nura AI is a web app that helps Muslims read the Quran, understand it through classical tafsir and AI-assisted study, and connect with authentic duas for real life — including finding the right supplication based on how they feel.

It is not a chatbot with a Muslim skin. The Quran, tafsir, and dua library are the product. AI sits on top of those sources to make them easier to study and more personal to use.

In one sentence: Nura is a beautifully crafted Islamic study companion — a full Quran reader, classical tafsir you can ask questions about, and an authentic dua library with AI that helps you find and understand the right words.

What Nura is not

  • Not a fatwa service, “AI imam,” or replacement for a scholar
  • Not a memorization / hifz error-correction app
  • Not a generic ChatGPT wrapper
  • Not only a Quran recitation player

Two layers, one brand

Everything lives at nura-ai.app. There are two related products. A marketer should understand both, even if most public content is about the consumer app.

Consumer

Nura AI

Core features

1. Quran Reader

A full digital mushaf experience for all 114 surahs and 6,236 verses.

  • Uthmani Arabic script, designed to look like a real Quran on screen
  • Word-by-word translation — tap any Arabic word to see its meaning
  • Multiple English (and other) translations
  • Audio recitation from renowned qaris (for example Mishary Rashid Alafasy)
  • Tajweed color-coding in light mode, with an on-screen legend (ghunnah, qalqalah, madd, and others)
  • Mushaf page view (like a printed Quran) or translation / verse-list view
  • Search by surah or verse content, bookmarks, reading history, light and dark themes

The reader is the visual heart of the product. Core reading is free.

2. Tafsir Explorer

Study classical commentary verse by verse — currently including works such as Tafsir Ibn Kathir — in a dedicated reader, not a dump of text.

  • Browse editions and read alongside the Arabic and translation
  • Highlights, notes, and bookmarks across tafsir
  • Continue-reading so you pick up where you left off
  • Share a verse as a branded image (Arabic, translation, optional tafsir excerpt)

3. AI Tafsir Chat (Premium)

On a given verse, the user can ask a question and receive an explanation grounded in classical tafsir — context, scholarly insight, how verses connect. It is a study aid, not a source of Islamic legal rulings.

Good examples of questions: “What is the context of this verse?” “What does Ibn Kathir say here?” “How does this ayah relate to the next?”

It should never be positioned as issuing fatwas, relationship advice, or medical/legal guidance.

4. Dua library

A curated collection of 100+ authentic duas with Arabic, translation, transliteration, and virtues, drawn from established sources (for example Sahih Bukhari where applicable). Built for daily use — morning, evening, protection, gratitude, and more.

5. Emotional Dua Search (Premium)

The user types how they feel — anxious, grateful, seeking guidance, heartbroken — and Nura matches authentic duas to that state. This is one of the most human, distinctive parts of the product: it starts from the person, then returns to the Sunnah, with the source on the card.

6. Dua Insights (Premium)

Deeper explanation of a chosen dua: meaning, related Quranic guidance, prophetic example, and how to live it — not just the text.

Nura Academy

A learning layer for Islamic education, especially Quran teachers who today run classes through WhatsApp, notebooks, or memory.

  • Teacher dashboard: classes and 1-on-1 students
  • Assignments tied to a surah/ayah range, completed inside the in-app Quran reader
  • Students can submit recitation audio
  • Gradebook with scores and a tajweed / fluency / accuracy rubric, plus written feedback
  • Progress tracking and a parent view of the child’s work
  • Calendar and video calling for virtual lessons

How Nura is different

People already know Quran.com, Muslim Pro, Tarteel, and generic AI chat. Nura’s distinction:

  • Vs. most Quran apps: we do not stop at reading. You can tap a word, hear a reciter, open classical tafsir, and ask a question on that verse.
  • Vs. hifz apps: we are not primarily about catching memorization mistakes. We are study, reflection, and dua — plus a classroom for teachers.
  • Vs. ChatGPT: the AI is attached to a verse and to tafsir, inside a product built for Muslims. It is not a blank chat box.

A useful way to hold it: Read. Listen. Understand. Ask. Make dua. For teachers: Assign. Hear. Grade.

Guardrails

These matter for any public explanation of Nura, including social content.

  • AI is a study companion grounded in classical tafsir. Always say that. Never “AI scholar” or “replaces your sheikh.”
  • Do not demo fiqh rulings, marriage advice, medical claims, or politics.
  • When a dua is shown, keep the source visible. Do not imply a dua “cures” anxiety or illness.
  • Quran recitation should be treated with respect — not under a beat, not as a joke, not stitched to comedy audio.
  • Arabic should be taken from the app, not retyped from memory.
  • Do not invent user numbers. “Thousands of Muslims” is the language already used on the site. Do not claim millions or scholarly endorsements you do not have.
  • Do not dunk on other Muslim apps. Show Nura.
