Surah Aal-e-Imran, Verse 97

فِيهِ ءَايَـٰتٌۢ بَيِّنَـٰتٌۭ مَّقَامُ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ ۖ وَمَن دَخَلَهُۥ كَانَ ءَامِنًۭا ۗ وَلِلَّهِ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ حِجُّ ٱلْبَيْتِ مَنِ ٱسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًۭا ۚ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَنِىٌّ عَنِ ٱلْعَـٰلَمِينَ
"In it are clear signs [such as] the standing place of Abraham. And whoever enters it shall be safe. And [due] to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House - for whoever is able to find thereto a way. But whoever disbelieves - then indeed, Allah is free from need of the worlds."
NuraAI Explains (Based on Tafsir Ibn Kathir):

The verse 3:97 of Surah Aal-e-Imran discusses the significance of the Kabah in Makkah as the first House appointed for mankind for acts of worship and religious rituals. It mentions that Ibrahim Al-Khalil built this sacred structure by Allah's command, inviting people to perform Hajj there. The verse highlights that the Kabah is full of blessings and serves as a guidance for all humanity.

According to Ibn Kathir's tafsir, the Maqam (station) of Ibrahim refers to the place where Prophet Ibrahim stood while building the Kabah with his son Isma'il. This station used to be next to the Kabah but was later moved slightly eastward during \`Umar bin Al-Khattab's reign for better access during Tawaf.

Furthermore, it emphasizes that whoever enters this sacred area attains security, making Makkah a safe refuge historically even during Jahiliyyah times. The verse also stresses on performing Hajj as a duty owed to Allah by those who are able to undertake this journey, highlighting its importance in Islam.
